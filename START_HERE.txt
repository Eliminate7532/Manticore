MANTICORE ALPHA - START HERE
============================

Thanks for trying Manticore. It is a free Commander playtester: you play Magic: The Gathering Commander
games against 1 to 3 AI opponents on your own PC, or against a friend online. The rules engine is Forge.

This is an ALPHA, so things will break. When they do, a report from you (F8, see part 5) is the most
useful thing you can send.


1. WHAT YOU NEED
----------------
- Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, on an ordinary (Intel or AMD) PC. Or Windows 11 on an Arm PC, such as a
  Surface Pro with a Snapdragon: it runs there through Windows' own translation, perhaps more slowly.
- 8 GB of RAM. It runs with less, but more slowly: under 8 GB the deck screen shows an orange note and
  the rules engine gets a smaller memory limit.
- About [500 MB] of free disk space, plus room for card pictures (they are kept once downloaded).
- An internet connection the first time you open a deck, to download card pictures from Scryfall.
- You do NOT need Python, Java or administrator rights. The installer brings everything it needs.


2. INSTALL, UPDATE, UNINSTALL
-----------------------------
Install
  1. Run Manticore-[x.y.z]-setup.exe from the link Karl sends you.
  2. The installer isn't signed yet, so Windows may say "Windows protected your PC". Click More info,
     then Run anyway.
  3. Accept the licence (GNU GPL v3), keep the suggested folder, and click Install. Leave "Run Manticore"
     ticked on the last page.
  Manticore installs for your Windows user only, and adds a Start menu shortcut.

Update
  When a new version is out, the title screen shows "A new version is ready". Click Update now:
  Manticore downloads it, closes, installs it and opens again by itself. Later and Skip this version
  work too (Cog > Updates turns the check off).
  You can also install a new version over the old one by hand. Either way your decks, settings and saved
  game are kept: they live in your own data folder (Cog > Open my data folder), not in the program folder.

Uninstall
  Windows Settings > Apps > Installed apps > Manticore > Uninstall. It asks "Also delete my decks, settings
  and saved games?". No (the default) keeps them for a later reinstall; Yes removes them too.


3. YOUR FIRST GAME
------------------
  1. Start Manticore and click Play on the title screen.
  2. On the deck screen, pick YOUR DECK. There are six starter decks. To use your own: copy its text list
     (in Moxfield: open the deck, Export, copy the text), click Import a deck, then Paste from clipboard.
     Ctrl+V on the deck screen does the same.
  3. Choose 1, 2 or 3 opponents and a deck for each AI: click its box, then a deck. Random and Same as mine
     work too.
  4. Click Start. The rules engine takes 10 to 20 seconds to wake up; the commanders face off while it does.
  5. Keep or mulligan your hand.
  6. A short tour of the table starts at your first chance to act: 12 steps, about a minute. It points at
     each part of the screen and its keys. It is not a Magic lesson. Esc skips it, and
     Cog > Tour of the table shows it again any time.

Keys worth knowing
  Space           OK, pass priority once, move on (the bar above your hand says what it will do)
  Enter           Pass until an opponent acts, or the turn ends
  Shift+Enter     Pass the rest of this turn (you are still asked to block)
  Ctrl+Shift      Full control on or off: every stop, nothing passed for you
  Esc             Cancel
  E               End turn
  A               Full Send: attack with everything that can
  U / Ctrl+Z      Take back a land you just tapped for mana
  M               Sound on / off
  + / -           Bigger / smaller text
  H               Every key and control
  F8              Report a bug, or suggest an idea

Formats
  The deck screen's Format buttons (top right) switch between Commander and Brawl (MTG Arena's 100-card
  Brawl: a creature or planeswalker commander, 25 life, 30 with 2-3 opponents, no commander damage). Import
  an Arena deck with its Export button, then Ctrl+V on the deck screen. Online play is Commander only.

Deck screen tags
  NOT LEGAL means a deck breaks a rule of its format (a banned card, the wrong colours, too many or too few
  cards...). The deck screen lists the lines; you can still play it. The one exception: a commander that
  can't legally be a commander (or two that can't be partners) stops the deck from starting.
  UNCHECKED means the card data needed to check hasn't arrived yet.

Card art
  Card art (deck screen) lets you choose the printing of each card in one of your decks. Pictures of your
  own go in the my_art folder inside your data folder, named after the card ("Sol Ring.png").
  Its MPC Autofill tab shows community-made pictures of a card from mpcfill.com; only the one you click is
  downloaded, and it is kept. "Card size" - and + make the pictures smaller or bigger.

Stats
  Stats (deck screen) shows a deck's record - games won and lost, by number of players, by your seat, and
  against each deck - on which of your turns you win and lose, and how (what beat you). Only games played to
  the end count; conceded and unfinished ones are listed apart. Online games are a separate record. The
  end-of-game screen says how the game ended and the deck's record so far.

Resume
  If the program closes mid-game, the next start offers Continue (title screen) or Resume last game (deck
  screen). A game saved by an older version can't be resumed; you'll be offered Discard instead.


4. PLAYING A FRIEND ONLINE (1 v 1)
----------------------------------
  One of you hosts, the other joins. The game runs on the host's PC.
  Host:  on the deck screen pick your deck, click Host online, then send your friend the invite code
         (Copy invite code) by message.
  Join:  pick your deck, click Join online, and paste the invite code.
  Both of you should have the same version of Manticore.
  Windows Firewall may ask about Java the first time you host: allow it.
  If the host screen says the router can't open the way (often "CGNAT"), the simplest fix is Tailscale
  (free) on both PCs; the host screen fills in the address when Tailscale is running.
  An online game can't be saved or resumed yet: if either side closes, the game ends.


5. WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG: F8
--------------------------------
Press F8 at any time, even with a question on screen. Write what happened and what you expected, in a
sentence or two, then send.

What a report contains
  - a picture of the table and the board (every card the game showed you)
  - the game log and every click since the game began
  - the deck lists of that game
  - the program's own logs, the versions of Manticore and Forge, your Windows version and screen size
  - the name you typed in the form, if any

What it does not contain
  - your Windows user name: folder paths are cut down to "~"
  - your computer's name
  - any of your files other than the program's own logs and the game's decks

Reports go to Karl's alpha Discord channel. If sending fails, the report is saved as a zip file (the
message says where): send that file to Karl instead.

If the program crashes or freezes, the next start shows a red line: "closed unexpectedly last time".
Click Send report, or Look first to see the zip before it goes. Nothing is ever sent without you clicking.

Have an idea instead? The F8 window has a second tab, "Suggest a feature": say what you would like and
what it would help you do. It sends only what you write (and a picture of the screen if you tick the box).
On the deck screen F8 opens straight on that tab.


6. KNOWN ISSUES (no need to report these)
-----------------------------------------
- The AI plays combo decks as midrange. It never goes infinite; scripted combo lines come later.
- An AI that starts a pointless loop is stopped after 10 repeats of one ability in a turn.
- The starter decks aren't balanced against each other. They are practice opponents in different styles.
- One table freezes the engine before turn 1: Valgavoth + Light-Paws + Kinnan + Ojer Axonil together. The
  deck screen warns you first.
- A "reorder" window every draw step when two of your own triggers happen together. That is Forge.
- The newest sets may be missing: the alpha uses one fixed Forge version. A card it doesn't know is named on
  the deck screen ("not in this version of Forge yet") and left out of the game, never silently swapped.
- At 200% text the screen is tight: some labels are shortened.
- [Anything Karl's 20 test games turn up.]
